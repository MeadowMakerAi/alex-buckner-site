One product, start to finish

I’m developing Sweet Plum gummies for Meadow Maker in New York. The pouch and the round tin carry the same photograph. To choose between them, I need to know what each one costs to fill.

The flavor and the pack

In the Sweet Plum studies, a black-and-white photograph of a crowd sits beside a muted plum strip. The photograph gives the pack its character. The strip gives someone looking across a shelf a place to find the flavor.

On the pouch, I have to leave room for the zipper and tear notch. On the tin, the picture and flavor name have to read on a round lid. I also need a place for the lot information that changes with each batch.

Making room for the cure

I’m planning the line around a pectin base. A depositor fills silicone molds. The gummies then need time on racks before they can be packed.

In the floor model, yesterday’s batch still takes up space when today’s comes off the line. A faster depositor can fill another tray. It can’t free up a rack. That’s why I plan the curing room alongside the machines.

The cost of a finished pack

Both formats need an accurate count. With a pouch, I can plan a feed from the counter into the open bag. A tin needs a lid and another handling step if it goes inside an outer pouch.

I start with the pre-tax shelf price and work back through the retailer’s margin and distribution. Then I can see how much of what we earn on a pack would go toward the gummy, the package and someone’s time at the table. A quote for an empty tin only gives me part of that answer.

What I’d test next

The work so far is a set of packaging studies and a production plan. I’d run both formats at the same packing table, then open the samples and check the count and condition of the gummies.

I’d keep the timed result alongside the supplier’s quote in FM Command. Back at the shelf, I’d photograph each pack from a shopper’s distance to see whether I can still read “Sweet Plum.”
