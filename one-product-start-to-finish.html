One product, start to finish

I’m developing Sweet Plum gummies for Meadow Maker in New York. The pouch and the round tin carry the same photograph. I want to know what fitting a lid adds to the cost of a pack, and whether the photograph still works when I crop it to a circle.

The flavor and the pack

Sweet Plum is one flavor in Meadow Maker’s gummy line. In the packaging studies, a black-and-white photograph of a crowd under a tree sits beside a narrow plum strip. I keep the flavor name in that strip so you know where to look when the photograph changes.

The pouch gives me a flat rectangle to work with. On the tin I have to crop the same photograph to a circle and keep the flavor name clear of the rim. I still need space for the label information that changes with each batch.

From the mold to the packing table

I plan the gummy line around a pectin base. The depositor fills silicone molds. Once the pieces have set, they come out of the molds and go onto trays to cure. The molds can go back for cleaning. The trays are still occupied when the next batch arrives.

I used the floor model to follow a worker carrying a load. In one proposed layout, the cooker blocked the route. That layout failed the simulation. I could rule it out before buying or moving a machine.

What changes at the packing table

The gummy and the target count stay the same. The container changes what the operator handles and what can go wrong. These are the two routes I’d compare in a packing trial.

The cost of a finished pack

A quote for an empty tin gives me one cost. To decide whether it works, I also need the cost of the pieces inside, the time to fill and close it, and how many packs need rework.

I work back from the shelf price through the retailer’s margin and distribution. That tells me what the business can spend on each pack. In FM Command, I keep the depositor and tin-filling quotes with the gummy project so I can trace those assumptions to their sources.

The first packing trial

So far I have packaging studies and a production plan. I’d take samples from the same cured batch, run both formats at the same table, and open the finished packs. I want to see which one holds up after filling and handling.

Then I’d step back to shelf distance and photograph both packs side by side. Can I still read “Sweet Plum” on the tin?
